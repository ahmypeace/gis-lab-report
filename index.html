GIS Lab Portfolio

Fifteen weeks of GIS problem solving at Temple University, from drinking-water risk in Flint to schools near landfills in Los Angeles and a site for a healthy food store in Philadelphia.

About This Portfolio

Peace Amaka Onebunne is a PhD student in communication at Temple University. This course treated GIS as a way to solve problems, and the three featured maps each ask where people face an environmental or access problem and where action should go first.

Featured Maps

Map 1 · Environmental risk

Who Is Most at Risk From Flint's Tap Water?

The question was where in Flint people are most at risk of drinking contaminated tap water. The analysis looked for places where three risks overlap: within 0.3 miles of a TRI facility releasing more than 10,000 tons of water pollution, within 0.3 miles of a river, and within 0.2 miles of a service line made of lead. Buffering each layer and intersecting the three results left a few areas that meet all three conditions. Four wards contain these areas, and Ward 5 holds the most, about 4.6 million square feet, so it should be screened first. The map shows where the risks overlap, not how many residents are exposed, so population data and water testing would be the next step.

Map 2 · Environmental justice

Schools Near Landfills in Los Angeles

The question was which lower-income neighborhoods in Los Angeles have schools close to methane-producing landfills. Of 811 schools in the city, 53 sit within 0.5 km of a landfill, and those schools fall in 29 census tracts. Splitting the tracts at their median income per capita of $23,674 left 14 lower-income tracts, all in Supervisorial Districts 1 and 2, with 29 schools and 19 nearby landfills. The result went into a letter to the First District Supervisor asking for air-quality monitoring and closer review of these sites. The map does not show that harm has occurred, but it points to places where children spend their days near a known hazard.

Map 3 · Site selection

Where Should a Healthy Food Store Go?

The task was to find land in Philadelphia for a new healthy food store that people could reach and that would not compete with existing healthy food outlets. Qualifying land had to sit inside an Empowerment Zone, within 2,000 feet of a SEPTA subway or Regional Rail station, and outside 1,200 feet of any Farmers Market or Healthy Corner Store. Layering those three rules left eight candidate areas in West and North Philadelphia. Candidate 4 is the largest at about 219.68 acres, so it is the recommended site. The map is a screen, so the next step would be checking the parcels inside Candidate 4 for zoning, ownership, and vacancy.
